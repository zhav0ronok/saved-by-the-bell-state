### 1.3.1 - What is an Operator?
In the context of quantum mechanics, an operator $A$ acts on a state vector of a system $|\psi\rangle$ and produces a different state vector $| \phi\rangle$ as such:
$$
A|\psi\rangle = |\phi\rangle
$$

### 1.3.3 - Projection Operators

The formal definition[^1] of a projection operator is not as useful in this context as the one dimensional case, where $P_{j}=|\phi_{j}\rangle \langle \phi_{j}|$. In the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ case, these operators are:
$$
P_{+}=|+\rangle\langle+|:=\begin{pmatrix}
1\\0
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
1&0
\end{pmatrix}= \begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\0&0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
P_{-}=|-\rangle \langle -| := \begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\0&1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
Where the operation above is an outer product. 


In general, in linear algebra, the projection of a vector $\mathbf{u}$ onto $\mathbf{v}$ (essentially, rotating $\mathbf{u}$ and putting it onto $\mathbf{v}$) is given by:
$$
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}= \left( \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|^2} \right)\mathbf{v}
$$

### Terminology

> [!NOTE] Positive Operator
> A positive operator $A$ has $\langle \psi | A | \psi\rangle \geq 0$ for all $|\psi\rangle$. This operator is *positive definite* if there is the additional constraint that $|\psi\rangle \neq 0$.

> [!NOTE] Support
> The *support* of an operator $A$ is defined as the vector space orthogonal to its kernel.

[^1]: A projector $P_{\mathscr{U}}$ on $\mathscr{U}$ is given by $P_{\mathscr{U}}|\Psi\rangle_{\mathscr{W}}=|\psi\rangle_{\mathscr{U}}$
